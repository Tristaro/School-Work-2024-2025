### Division of Work

For this project, I was responsible for analyzing **Media as Technology**, focusing on how microtransactions are engineered into game design, and **Media as a Commodity**, discussing how microtransactions have become a dominant business model. In addition to my research and writing, I assisted in script writing, helped create the presentation, and took on the task of making the video.

The work was divided this way because my partner, Tuan Minh, preferred to work on **Media as Text**, while I was flexible with the remaining topics. Since I ended up covering two of the three topics, I had to do more reading, but I didn’t mind because the sources I used for one topic were often relevant to the other. We both contributed to script writing to ensure proper timing and detail, and we worked on the presentation separately due to our limited chances to meet in person. I took charge of video production because I have two years of experience in digital media from high school which I thought could be useful here.

Overall, I think the division of labor was effective since we managed to complete the project successfully. However, I did feel like I had to read more, but it was manageable due to the overlap in research sources.

### Collaboration Process

Scheduling was a major challenge due to **midterms**, **reading week**, and **living far from each other**. These factors limited our ability to meet in person, so we only had two face-to-face meetings: once during the tutorial on the first day of the project and again on **Friday, the 28th**.

Despite this, we maintained communication online through **Discord**, using both messages and calls. We used **Google Docs** for shared writing, **Google Slides** for our presentation, and Discord for planning and general communication.

### Challenges & Solutions

The biggest challenge was **time management** due to **midterms** and **reading week**. Because our schedules were packed with studying, we had limited availability to meet. Even finding time on **Friday the 28th** was difficult, but we managed to meet during a **three-hour gap** when I was waiting for my next class at the **north campus building**.

To overcome this, we worked separately on our individual parts and then used our in-person meeting to combine our work and finalize the shared portions of the project.

### Impact of Technology on Collaboration

Using **Discord** was helpful for planning our meetings, especially since we only had one scheduled class together (tutorial), and I wasn’t able to attend one of them. However, online collaboration was quite different from working in person.

One major difference was that online, we couldn’t easily see each other’s progress in real-time, which made it harder to ensure that our work aligned. While we communicated our direction and progress, we couldn’t immediately address **overlaps** or **contradictions** in our research. In contrast, when working in person, we could actively **discuss, adjust, and time our speaking segments**, which was especially helpful when recording to meet the **five-minute limit**.

### Lessons Learned & Future Improvements

Through this experience, I learned that **early scheduling** is crucial for better time management. If I could redo this project, I would **schedule group meetings within the first few days** and **complete the shared work immediately**, so that we wouldn’t have to stress about meeting near the deadline. I think this would help avoid last-minute coordination issues and ensure smoother collaboration overall.
